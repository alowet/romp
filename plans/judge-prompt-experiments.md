## Why now

The user's decision (2026-09-20, paraphrased): Completed must be safe to clear without reading. Nothing a session left undone, offered as a next step, or asked the user about may land under Completed; all of that belongs in the category becoming Needs you. The judges' prompts decide these placements, so the change is a prompt change. Prompt changes move cards, every card move claims something changed, and the cards-move rule (CLAUDE.md) forbids moves on inference flaps; so the user wants a quantitative experiment on their own history before any prompt tweak lands: how many offers, open questions and undone items reach Completed under the current prompt and under each candidate, how many genuinely finished threads a candidate would pull into Needs you (a false interrupt is a broken flow state), how many cards flap between builds, and what each pass costs.

## The premise, checked in code

- **Where the prompts live.** `kernel/judge.py`: `PLAN_SYS` (the planner, one segment against the open-goals menu; ops `done`, `block`, `awaiting` and the tree edits), `CLOSER_SYS` (the closer, a turn-end auditor over the open goals the turn worked on; per goal `done`, `blocked` or kept open), `UNBLOCK_SYS` (the unblocker, whether a blocked goal's question was since answered or made moot). The model calls are `plan_llm(segment_text, menu_text, model=, effort=)`, the closer's call inside `_close_turn`, and `unblock_llm(blocks_text, since_text, completed_text)`. The prompts are module constants read at call time, so a harness can swap a candidate in by assigning the module attribute for the run, with no code path of its own.
- **What the current prompt already says, and where it points the other way.** `CLOSER_SYS` rules that a turn closing with a question that awaits the user's go-ahead is not done, and files the goal owning that decision as blocked. It also rules the opposite for one shape: a goal whose only loose end is an explicitly optional offer is done, not blocked. Under the user's decision that shape is exactly what must leave Completed, so the first candidate prompt is the closer's with that clause inverted; the planner's `done` op and the unblocker's moot rule carry the same question for offers ("an offer or approval whose work was then done anyway" is moot) and are the second and third surfaces.
- **How a verdict becomes a column.** Every verdict goes through `record_verdict(store, node, src, kind, ...)`: an append to the node's verdict log and the flags; `rollup_status` derives a status per TOP-LEVEL card (`completed`, `blocked`, `working` or `cleared`; `awaiting` is a planner op and a closer verdict kind kept as an annotation on the node, which the feed shows as a flavor of Working, never a status value); the feed (`kernel/kernel.py`, build_feed's card loop) files a top under `needs_input` when the status is `blocked` (or an API block, a permission prompt, a stall), under `completed` when the status is `completed`, skips a `cleared` top (no card), else `working`; a recheck or a rejudging after a reply, the live awaiting snapshot and a peer wait can move a blocked top to Working, inputs a kernel-less harness does not have. So the harness computes the store-derivable part of the rule (`blocked` to `needs_input`, `completed` to `completed`, `cleared` its own bucket, else `working`) and names the live-only inputs as out of its reach. One session-state input rides the rollup: `rollup_status(store, session_closed)` completes the focus top (the top of `lastNode`) only when the session is settled, so the harness passes `_session_settled` over the ending's own truncated transcript, the closest reading to what the live judges had (the reviewer's option c), and pins that an ending whose own top the closer marks done reads `completed`.
- **What the user's past card actions left behind.** The override journal `STATE/overrides/<fsid>.jsonl` (`append_override`, `append_clear`) carries the user's gestures as append-only events: on this machine today, 640 `clear` rows by the user (the cross-off), 465 `followup` rows (the user re-opened a card with more to do), 22 `resolve`, 23 `unclear` and 10 `restore` (a cleared card brought back); the verdict logs add 11 user `done` and 10 user `clear` events; the 337 `block` rows are the kernel's (the interrupt and nudge roads), not the user's. A `followup` on a card the judges had completed is the strongest recorded signal of a false Completed; a `clear` with no later `followup`, `unclear` or `restore` on the same card is the recorded signal of an accepted one. Neither is a clean label of "offer" against "finished", so the labelling section below uses them as the first tier and a labeller pass as the second.
- **What exists to run the judges outside the kernel.** `romp-judge --plan` and `--close` run one tier over the sessions `discover` finds under the state root; `_rebind_state(path)` repoints the goal, caption, episode and archive directories to another root; `ROMP_CLAUDE_BIN` names the model binary the calls use; `_ab_close_session` already sweeps every turn of one session through the closer on a deep copy of its store, oldest first, and reports the completed tops before and after without saving anything; `_ab_classify` already runs the planner's blocked-or-working verdict under arms of model and effort and diffs against the live status without mutating it. Every model call lands a row in `judge-usage.jsonl` with its judge, model, milliseconds and cost, which is the spend ledger the harness reads back.
- **What a pass costs today** (the last seven days on this machine, from `judge-usage.jsonl`): closer 3169 calls at a mean of 0.11 dollars and 13 seconds each; planner 2169 at 0.08 dollars and 12 seconds; unblocker 684 at 0.05 dollars and 6 seconds. The corpus: 85 registered sessions, 115 goal stores holding 883 top-level cards and 1211 nodes, 68 override journals.

## The corpus

Built once by a script under the harness, from the live state root read only, into `~/.cache/romp-judge-experiments/corpus/<date>/`, never into the repository and never into a mail or a body. An **ending** is one finished turn of one session: the transcript up to that turn's result, the goal store (with its goals-archive) cut to the turn's start and re-keyed to the ending id. The corpus writes no record of the placements the live judges filed; tier one reads the user's later gestures from the live root through `--live-state` at label time, so it exists only while that root still lists the session. Endings are the turns the model worked (a bare slash command or an interrupted turn is not one), selected in four classes by a heuristic pre-pass over the turn's last non-command assistant text, then stratified by the labelling below:

1. **Offers**: the turn ends by offering a next step it did not take ("I can also", "if you want, I could", "shall I").
2. **Open questions**: the turn ends by asking the user something it needs answered.
3. **Undone items**: the turn's own to-do list or the transcript shows work it named and left (an unchecked item, a "not done" line, a test it did not run).
4. **Finished threads** (the control): the turn delivers what was asked and states so, with no offer, no question and no undone item.

Target size: 300 endings, roughly 75 per class, drawn across sessions and weeks so no one session dominates; a pilot of 60 (15 per class) runs first. The heuristics are the selection, not the truth: every selected ending gets a label.

## Labelling: a stratification frame, not a truth about the ending

The pilot showed that the user's own action on a card is not a truth about the ending's SHAPE: every disagreement ran one way, the user cleared a card (a "finished" gesture) whose last turn the labeller read as an open question, an undone item or an offer (agreement 6 of 19, 31.6 percent). That is the habit the change serves, not a labeller fault. So the two are separated.

- **The labeller** (a strong model over the ending's last turn: its final assistant text and the user's ask that opened THAT turn, read from the event model's own segmentation of the ending turn's opener, the card titles not sent) answers one of the four classes with a one-line reason, run twice with the class order shuffled (never the same order twice). The ask is the ending turn's OWN opener, not the session's first user text: the per-ending transcript is the whole session truncated at the ending, and the base appended the session's first ask, so every ending past a session's first turn was labelled under an earlier turn's ask. The pilot's stability and agreement figures were produced under that bug and are re-measured by the labeller re-run this change carries (the figures are in the Status paragraph). Its label is the agreement of the two runs, and its OWN gate is STABILITY, not agreement with anything: the fraction of endings that get the same class in both orders must reach 90 percent. The pilot met the gate at exactly the boundary; a full run BELOW it does not drop the experiment, it drops the class column (the measures score against the user's actions regardless) and the labeller is re-prompted and re-run for the stratification frame. The class is a stratification frame only ("tier one" is the user's own recorded verdict, the ground truth; the labeller's class is the frame): it groups endings so a leak or a false interrupt can be read per class, never a ground truth a measure is scored against.
- **The user's own recorded actions** are the ground truth the measures use (below). They need no labeller: they are the events the journals hold.

## The harness

A script under `scripts/` (or a `--experiment` road in the judge module, whichever the read prefers) that, per arm:

1. Copies the corpus into a fresh scratch state root under `~/.cache/romp-judge-experiments/runs/<run>/<arm>/` and rebinds the judge module onto it (`_rebind_state`), so no run reads or writes the live root; the kernel is never started; `ROMP_CLAUDE_BIN` stays the real binary for paid passes and the tests' fake for the harness tests.
2. Swaps the arm's prompt into the module attribute (`CLOSER_SYS`, `PLAN_SYS`, `UNBLOCK_SYS`: the current text for the baseline arm, a candidate file for each other arm).
3. For each ending, rolls the seeded copy up once and saves it (so the planner's first menu reads the flags, not a stripped cache), runs the planner over the turn's segments and the closer over the turn with the goal-history map production sends (as `_close_session` builds it; the `_ab_close_session` precedent omits it), then the unblocker over the goals the pass blocked; records each top's rolled-up status and its column by the store-derivable rule above, with four outcomes (`completed`, `needs_input`, `working`, `cleared`), and whether the top is SCORED for this ending: born, or given a `done` or `block` verdict, inside the ending's turn. The grouper and consolidator are DISABLED for arm runs (the planner calls the grouper after every placement in production; the harness no-ops `_group_store` and `_consolidate_store`): they are not among the three measured judges, their non-deterministic reshaping of the top set was the pilot's largest flap component, and the grouper's stuck calls were its 120s-alarm timeouts. This is a systematic difference from production, applied EQUALLY to every arm, so the arms' comparison (what the measure needs) is not biased one arm against another; it does bias the ABSOLUTE counts down, since a top a live grouper would have split out inside the ending's own turn is not a top in the arm, so the suffix join can miss a user gesture on such a node (bounded to grouper actions within the turn, and the same for every arm). One more per-call departure the harness could pin but does not: every arm-judge call mints a fresh security mark (the judge module's `_mark`, a CSPRNG token in the system prompt and every content section), so the three builds' inputs are IDENTICAL only up to that mark; the mark rides every production call too, so it is no harness-side bias, but the residual flap mixes model sampling with any sensitivity to the mark, and pinning a per-ending mark constant across arms (as the grouper-off is a disclosed departure) is the lever that would separate them. Every failed call or rejected reply is counted (`failures`) and marks the arm's row not comparable EXCEPT one the ledger attributes to a non-arm reshaping or summarizing judge (those named in `NON_ARM_JUDGES`): the count is an EXCLUSION list, not an allowlist of the arm judges, because the arm also runs the opener and the placer and some failure kinds carry a tier or `romp` rather than a judge name, all of which an allowlist would wrongly drop; an excluded row is not lost, the report carries its count beside the comparable cell (`nonArmFailures`). The store copy an ending starts from is the live store cut to the ending's turn start: `rompUuid` and every id prefix re-keyed to the ending id (placements, closed turns, trails), the ending turn's own work-run placement dropped so the arm plans it once, its prompt-run placement and node kept, nodes and verdicts from the turn or later dropped, the sealing fields dropped, gate stamps above the cut dropped. Claude's task store is not copied: the arms reconstruct the to-do state from the transcript, where production read the store. A top the user cleared before the ending's turn opened rides its clear log to a `cleared` column in the seed; one cleared inside the turn is open at the seed, the arm's to rule on. A store the readers cannot parse is counted (`skipped["store-unreadable"]` in the builder, a `tierOneError` per session hash in the labeller's summary), never read as "nothing applies".
4. Runs the same ending THREE times from the same store copy. Each card gets a VALUE PER BUILD: the scored column where that build scored it, else an `_UNSCORED` sentinel. A card's column for scoring is a real column ONLY on a STRICT MAJORITY of the three builds (its count more than half), else no column (the sentinel): a card scored in a minority of builds, an all-different set, and any tie (two builds one each, or three different columns) all win no column and score no leak; a real 2-of-3 wins. No single build decides, so it is order-independent (the earlier first-seen tie rule let the first build decide, the 2026-09-22 PR 2035 review); the flap figure is the residual disagreement, a card whose per-build values are not all equal, the sentinel included. The majority is what the leak, false-interrupt and answered-then-cleared measures read, so a single build's sampling noise no longer decides a card's verdict, and the flap figure names the noise that remains. The build count and this rule are STAMPED in each arm's `results.json` (`buildsPerCard`) and in the report table, so a reader knows the columns are a majority of N and the flap the residual.
5. Before the first ending, the auth preflight runs the judges' OWN road once (built from `_judge_cmd` and `_judge_env` with the resolved billing, so it strips the ambient credential and injects only the resolved login tokens exactly as a judge call does) and is the SOLE gate: it refuses on an error envelope, a "Not logged in" reply or a missing session id, quoting the CLI's own words. There is no hard apiKeyHelper-file refusal (it false-refused a login-road operator whose helper the corpus does not carry). When it refuses, the remedy is to HAND-PLACE the helper file into the existing corpus's claude root, never to rebuild: a rebuild re-picks the endings and orphans the labeller's id-keyed labels. The corpus builder copies only a USER apiKeyHelper into the corpus claude root, resolved through the credentials module in Claude Code's own precedence; a MANAGED helper is read by the child from the system path and is not copied.
6. Sums the arm's cost from the `judge-usage.jsonl` rows the run appended under its own root.

**Seeds plan the ending's own turn, independent of the placements version (method change, 2026-09-23).** The planner's placement identity carries a version (`PLACEMENTS_V`), and `_plan_session` SEALS a store whose version is older whole (`_migrate_placements`, the dormant-session replay guard): every unplaced unit becomes processed-with-no-goal, so no arm judge plans anything. The corpus seeds sit at heterogeneous OLD versions (a session's store is only migrated when the live kernel plans it, so dormant sessions keep the version they were last planned under). This bit the experiment when `PLACEMENTS_V` went 14 to 15 on main (2026-09-23, the parallel-tool-batch change): the first paid re-pilot read both arms comparable while the PLANNER made ZERO calls, because every seed sealed. It also means the 2026-09-22 clean run's flap figure was PARTIAL: only the ~30 seeds then at the current version (14) planned, the rest were already sealed, and the version-14 seeds replayed some un-deduped history, inflating flaps. So **the clean run's 44/45 is WITHDRAWN as a baseline**; a corrected run's figures stand alone. The fix (`seal_pre_cut_adopt`): before planning each ending, SEAL every ready unit born BEFORE the ending's cut (keyed on the unit's TIME, which is derivation-independent and so survives the next version bump) and ADOPT the current version, so the arm plans exactly the ending's OWN turn ("history already planned, plan the turn once", the fairest stand-in for a live session that kept up; a full adoption would replay history and measure flaps the experiment does not care about). The fix also handles an OPENER-LESS ending (a continuation, `startT` None): its seed cut is the previous turn's end (`seedStart` in the manifest), so the run seals and adopts it the same way rather than skipping it and leaving `_plan_session` to seal it whole. And a **comparability precondition**: an arm in which a REQUIRED judge (`planner` or `closer`, each run for nearly every judged ending) made ZERO model calls over the whole arm is NOT comparable, named as such in the table's failures cell, so a silent judge can never read comparable again. The `placer` and `unblocker` are REPORTED in the per-judge counts but not required, because both are conditional (a sub-step's placer call, the unblocker over a pass's blocks), so a zero in one is a reading, not a harness fault; and a results record with no per-judge counts at all (an old, withdrawn run) reads not comparable rather than passing on an empty cell. A degenerate opener-less ending with no previous turn to seal on is refused loudly (a `no-seed-cut` failure that counts against comparability), never sealed on the cut. Beyond that whole-arm silence, a PER-ENDING precondition holds: an ending whose arm run made ZERO planner calls is named in `endingsUnplanned` and marks THAT arm not comparable; the corpus-wide excuse (the 2026-09-24 grounded rule) lifts this only when every arm planned nothing on that ending and its own final turn was grounded a workless one. Each arm-judge call is re-sampled up to three times at a 240-second alarm where production runs ONE attempt at 120 seconds; a timer kill re-draws the reply, and because a killed call's served duration tracks its output size, the kill falls more often on a LONG reply, so the arm with more kills keeps fewer of its long replies and files fewer done/block verdicts even while both arms read comparable: a SELECTION effect, symmetric across arms in mechanism but not in realization. The per-ending retry counts are recorded in `results['endings']` and the measure's `attribution`, and a killed attempt is billed but writes no usage row, so the budget stop counts it at the mean call cost. The leak and false-interrupt measures key their turn window on `seedStart` too (not `startT`), so an opener-less ending's measures are not structurally empty.

**The corpus-unplanned excuse grounds on the ending's own turn (2026-09-24).** An ending that planned nothing in EVERY expected arm is either a corpus property (its own final turn had nothing for the planner) or a harness fault that silenced the planner in every arm (a seed boundary at or past the cut seals the ending's own units; a dead planner). The report tells them apart by the ending's OWN content: it counts the units the ARM's planner plans with an unconditional planner MODEL CALL from a sealable own-turn position (work, live), from the parsed turn and the cut, NEVER the seed boundary, whose fault this catches; recorded per ending at run time and re-parsed from the read-only corpus for a run whose arms predate the field. An OPENER unit (a workless opening message) is the opener judge's, not the planner's, so it never counts. A NUDGE (a romp Nudge on a goal) is resolve-or-noop: it drives a planner call only on a still-working goal, so it is not excluded as never-plannable, but the seal reaches only PRE-CUT units, so an OWN-TURN nudge is never sealed, and an unplanned own-turn nudge is therefore one of the kernel's three no-call roads (no resolvable target in the store; the moot retirement when the goal is already done; the goal not open, e.g. view-cleared), not a missed call. So neither an opener nor a nudge counts, and an ending whose own turn holds only openers and nudges is excused; only a work or live unit left unplanned is a fault. A DELEGATION is also excluded, for a different reason: the ARM runs no courier, so a delegation unit reads its target unset and drives no planner call in ANY arm, a harness limitation symmetric across arms (delegation endings' planner behaviour is unmeasured by the experiment), named here as a residual. RESIDUAL (stated honestly): a nudge-only own turn cannot itself distinguish a MOOT nudge (goal already resolved) from a planner that was silenced on that ending; the seal cannot reach an own-turn nudge, and the arms' PLANNER CALL COUNTS (1000+ planner calls across the run) are the evidence the planner was alive, so a per-ending nudge-silence reads as moot, not a dead planner. The whole-arm silent-planner precondition still catches a globally dead planner. Zero planner units means nothing to plan: the ending is excused from the COMPARABILITY verdict only (it marks no arm not comparable), but it is STILL SCORED into every metric, because every excused ending sits in every arm's results, so the arms already compare on one set, and skipping it would drop the closer's and unblocker's verdicts the candidates exist to change. A candidate WITH planner units is a fault, not a workless turn: it is not excused, and every arm reads not comparable with the reason. The excuse needs a COMPLETE run: the expected arm set is recorded once in the run root (`arms.json`, written at launch and refused if a later launch names a different set), at least TWO arms, every one present and finished; a one-arm or partial run excuses nothing and the report labels itself partial (N of M arms finished).

File it blocked ... awaiting the user'; candidate_all ADDS to PLAN_SYS's `done` op 'A goal whose ONLY remaining loose end is an explicitly OPTIONAL offer ... is a block, not a done ... file block ... until the user says go or explicitly declines', and ADDS an exception to UNBLOCK_SYS's moot rule: the shipped sentence opens 'A goal the session has visibly moved past is moot even though nobody typed an answer: an offer or approval whose work was then done anyway (or a newer variant of it shipped), or a decision that later work made irrelevant'; candidate_all keeps that (a decision made irrelevant is still moot) and adds that an explicitly optional offer the user has not answered is NOT made moot by its work being done anyway, so it HOLDS. Rules the harness keeps: every run through `capped`; paid passes only inside a quiet window the manager holds and only after the manager's go-ahead on the run's budget; concurrency at the gear's setting; a run that exceeds its budget by a fifth stops and reports.

**Harness low queued (2026-09-23):** the `label` pass has no per-ending cache clear in its loop, so over a large corpus it accumulates and OOM-kills at the capped memory ceiling (it did at 15 GB over 300 endings on the 2026-09-23 full run; worked around by labelling in chunks of 60 and merging `labels.json`). This is now IMPLEMENTED (harness head d40016b57): a new event-model function `evict_document` drops a document's entries from BOTH caches (record via `_cache_pop_locked` under its lock, assembly via `_asm_release` outside its lock), called after each ending in the `label` loop AND in `run_arm_inprocess`, so both stay flat over the corpus. A record byte-budget alone does not help: the assembly cache still grows one entry per ending. The per-build `parse_cache_clear` did not bound the arms either (it empties the judge's parse cache, which these loops never fill); the observed arm peak before the fix was about 4.5 GB at 42 endings, ~105 MB per ending, projecting the 14 GB cap near ending 130. A second harness low, IMPLEMENTED in PR 2122 (the merged harness follow-up, merged AFTER this run's launch head d40016b5, which is why the in-flight arms predate it): `run_arm_inprocess` recorded only the prompt KEYS, so a results file could not identify which candidate text ran; it now stamps `promptHashes` (the sha256 of each prompt key's EFFECTIVE text after `apply_prompts`) and the harness commit into the results, so two runs whose baseline files match are still told apart if judge.py moved. The in-flight arms predate this stamp.

## The measures, per arm

The change is about the user's habit of clearing Completed without reading, so the measures are scored against the user's OWN later actions, not the labeller's class. For each ending the harness reads the live override journal for the gestures that POSTDATE the ending's CUT (the manifest's `cutT`, for every top the arm placed, live row or not: every arm verdict uses evidence up to the cut, and a closer `done`'s own `ev_t` is the turn's START, so a gesture made mid-turn before the card was placed must not read as a later one; the placement's `ev_t`/`at` and the arm's wall clock are both wrong keys): a `clear` on a card with no later `followup`, `unclear` or `restore` is the user treating it as finished; a `followup`, `unclear` or `restore` is the user re-opening it. "The card the user acted on" is matched to the top the arm placed by the node's SUFFIX (`g<N>`): the arm's results carry the ending id as the prefix (`<ending id>:g<N>`) and the live store carries the store key (`<rompUuid>:g<N>`), so the full keys never coincide by construction and the suffix is the only stable join; within one store every top-level node has a distinct suffix, so the join is unambiguous. When several cards move in the ending, each is scored on its own node's later gestures, independently. The store key is fixed in the manifest at corpus build, so a later move of the live session cannot change which store the measures read; a corrupt or unreadable store, archive or journal is recorded as a fault, never treated as "nothing applies".

1. **Leaks into Completed**: an ending with a later user gesture where the arm placed a top in `completed` that the user then re-opened (a `followup`, `unclear` or `restore` on that node after the cut). The card looked done to the arm and was not to the user. A `followup` is written for EVERY reply that cites a card (`askFollowUp` with an `itemId`): the citation chip, the card composer, a Continue, a Check status. A Continue on a Completed card IS a leak (the user reopened it to carry it on), and counts. A Check status is not a re-open, but its `followup` row is byte-identical to a real one (node, op, `t`, no text), so the measure CANNOT tell them apart; both count, and the leak figure carries an unknown Check-status share the measure cannot separate (a later kernel follow-up if a distinguishable row is added). Leaks are reported per class (below), and gated within the loose-ended strata. Must go to zero within those strata and stay at or below the baseline overall.
2. **False interrupts**: an ending where the arm left a top in `needs_input` that the user then plainly crossed off (a `clear`/`resolve` after the cut, with no `followup`/`unclear`/`restore` after) AND the kernel did not lift the block between the cut and that clear. The card sat in Needs you and the user just crossed it off. The answering turn the block needed is exactly the unblocker JUDGE's own ruling: the kernel lifts a block with an `unblock` event from several sources, and only ONE suppresses: the unblocker JUDGE's lift (`src` "unblocker", `kernel/judge.py`), which it stamps having ruled the block ANSWERED or MOOT (both under one why prefix). This is the exact event "no turn of the user's own answering it" approximated, read from the live store, not a re-scan of the transcript. The other `unblock` sources do NOT suppress: a topic-blind "you re-engaged" user unblock (the user typed anything), a reopen-ancestor lift (optimistic), the planner's new-work unblock, and any mechanical `romp` unblock. The user's reply through the card's box records its reply-unblock why on the block's DESCENDANTS, never on the top itself, so it needs no arm: a box reply on a top lands as a `followup` (a re-open). A judge lift AT or AFTER the clear does not count either (a same-second lift is not strictly after the cut, so a card whose examine the kernel stamped on the cut's own second reads conservatively as a false interrupt). A `needs_input` top the judge lifted (answered or moot) before the clear is counted separately as **answered then cleared** (the card did its job), never a false interrupt; whether that lift was answered versus moot is not separable from the current why (both carry one prefix), a later kernel follow-up if the share matters. Two corpus notes: a hideFromFeed mute journals a `src`-user clear per open top, and since a distinguishable why (`kernel/kernel.py`'s hidden-from-feed why) was added it is excluded, but a mute journaled BEFORE that change carries the generic cross-off why and is indistinguishable from the user's own, so it counts as one; the missed-ruling bias runs against every arm equally, since the live rulings are the same for all arms. False interrupts are reported BY CLASS (the finished stratum apart from the loose-ended offer/question/undone) beside the total, in `measures.json` (`falseInterruptsByClass`): a candidate's false interrupt within the labeller-finished stratum is the one that matters, and the loose-ended strata are where a real interrupt is expected. Must not rise above the baseline.
3. **Flaps**: cards whose per-build value is not the same across the THREE builds of the same ending (the settled gate is the same for every build, so only verdict differences count). Each build's value is the scored column or the `_UNSCORED` sentinel, so a card scored in some builds and not others flaps; the scoring column is the majority over all three, and the flap figure is the disagreement that majority leaves behind. Every ending contributes, with or without a later gesture.
4. **Cost per pass** in dollars, and the mean call time, from the ledger. Every ending contributes.

Endings with NO later user gesture (the journals record nothing after the cut) contribute to flaps and cost only; they cannot score a leak or a false interrupt, since there is no user action to compare. Two counts must not be confused. The GESTURE count is the endings with a `followup`/`unclear`/`restore` or a `clear`/`resolve` after the cut (`resolve` counts as a finished cross-off, the same as `clear`, both in `FINISHED_OPS`). The labeller's AGREEMENT DENOMINATOR is the smaller set of endings that carry BOTH a stable labeller label AND a tier-one verdict, since `tier_one_label` returns one collapsed label per ending and the labeller's summary drops its unstable reads; an ending with a gesture but no stable label, or no tier-one verdict, is not in it. The corpus does not draw at random: `build_corpus` takes the tier-one-eligible endings FIRST per class (an ending whose own turn a judge placed a card in), so the eligible endings are front-loaded and the gesture rate does NOT scale linearly to 300. The pilot's 60 endings were all tier-one-eligible (15 per class); the expected gesture endings at 300 must be derived from the eligible counts per class in the full corpus, not from the pilot's gesture rate. The pilot's own gesture and tier-one counts, re-measured under the cut key, are in the Status paragraph. Every failed call or rejected reply still marks the arm's row not comparable EXCEPT one the ledger attributes to a non-arm reshaping or summarizing judge (grouper, consolidator, distiller, gister): a stuck grouper call (the pilot's 120s-alarm timeouts), or a gister call `_followup_title` makes with no cached gist, is not an arm-judge fault. The count is an exclusion list, not an allowlist of the arm judges, because the arm also runs the opener and the placer and some failure kinds carry a tier or `romp` rather than a judge name.

A candidate lands only when, on the full corpus, its leaks are zero within the loose-ended strata (offer, question, undone) and at or below the baseline overall, its false interrupts within the labeller-finished stratum are at or below the baseline, and its flaps are at or below the baseline; the strata are read from the labeller's class as a frame; the bar reads ALL endings (there is no separate comparable denominator, and the corpus-unplanned excuse lifts only the comparability penalty, never removing an ending from a count). The report emits both keyings in `measures.json`: `leaksByClass` / `falseInterruptsByClass` keyed on the manifest's heuristic class, and `leaksByLabellerClass` / `falseInterruptsByLabellerClass` keyed on the labeller's own class (from `labels.json` when the labeller ran under the same run root), plus a per-ending `attribution` list (id, arm, leak, false interrupt, both class keyings), so the bar's sentence is derivable from the outputs under either keying. The result is a row in this note and the prompt change files as its own fix pull request with the figure (a cleanplots figure: horizontal bars from zero, one panel per measure, the arms as bars, the exact numbers annotated, counts and dollars only, never a line of the corpus).

## Roads not taken

- **A synthetic corpus.** Invented endings would measure the prompt against the author's idea of an offer, not the user's history; the tests use synthetic fixtures, the experiment uses the real endings, under `~/.cache/romp-judge-experiments` only.
- **An A/B on the live kernel.** The judges' passes would move the user's real cards under two prompts at once; the harness runs on copies and the kernel never sees them.
- **A prompt tweak first, measurement after.** The tweak is what moves cards; the measurement is the gate.
- **The labeller alone as the truth.** One model grading another shares its blind spots; the user's recorded actions come first, and the labeller's agreement with them is a DIAGNOSTIC that gates nothing (the labeller's own gate is stability alone). The agreement figure says how far the labeller's class tracks the user's habit, no more; it never decides whether a label counts.
- **Reusing `_ab_close` as it stands.** It sweeps the live root and prints titles to the terminal; the harness takes its sweep and drops both.
- **Tier one as a truth about the ending's class.** The pilot showed a user clear is not "the ending had no loose end"; it is the habit the change serves. Tier one drives the measures directly (the user's actions ARE the ground truth) and the labeller's class is a frame only.
- **Reading the harness's columns as the board's.** The board's column takes live inputs (an API block, a permission prompt, a stall, a recheck after a reply, the awaiting snapshot, a peer wait); the harness measures the judges' verdicts through the store-derivable rule and says so.
